## Current Areas of Interest

Australia faces a diverse range of long-term challenges, opportunities and risks.

National Backlog aims to provide a structured way to track these issues, measure progress, identify emerging risks and maintain institutional memory over time.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

### Society & Communities
- Housing Affordability
- Cost of Living
- Homelessness
- Domestic Violence
- Youth Crime
- Social Cohesion
- Loneliness and Social Isolation
- Indigenous Outcomes
- Population Ageing

### Health
- Regional Healthcare Access
- Mental Health
- GP Shortages
- Emergency Department Wait Times
- Disability Services
- Aged Care
- Preventative Health

### Education & Skills
- Teacher Shortages
- Literacy and Numeracy
- STEM Participation
- Vocational Education
- Workforce Skills Gaps
- Research and Innovation

### Economy
- Productivity Growth
- National Debt

- Small Business Viability

- Economic Resilience

### Infrastructure

### Technology

### Energy & Environment
- Energy Reliability
- Energy Affordability

### Governance
- Government Service Delivery
- Regulatory Complexity

- Evidence-Based Decision Making

### National Security
- Defence Readiness
- Supply Chain Resilience
- Biosecurity
- Critical Infrastructure Security
- Emergency Management

This is not an exhaustive list.

The purpose of National Backlog is to provide a scalable framework capable of tracking hundreds of interconnected national issues, their ownership, associated risks, and measurable progress over time.
