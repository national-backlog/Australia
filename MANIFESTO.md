# National Backlog
## A Public Register of Australia's Unresolved Challenges

### Manifesto v0.1

---

## Why We Exist

Australia has no shortage of debate.

Every day brings new headlines, new controversies, and new stories competing for attention.

Yet many of our most important challenges persist for years or decades. Public attention moves on, governments change, news cycles reset, and unresolved problems remain.

We believe there should be a better way.

Every successful organisation tracks its work through issue registers, risk registers, backlogs, owners, targets, and measurable outcomes.

A nation should be able to do the same.

National Backlog exists to create a public memory of the challenges we face and the progress we make in addressing them.

---

## What We Are

National Backlog is a public register of Australia's unresolved challenges.

It is:

- A long-term issue tracker
- A public record of national priorities
- A progress dashboard
- An evidence-based reference
- A shared civic resource

It is a place where citizens can answer simple questions:

- What are Australia's biggest unresolved issues?
- Are they improving or getting worse?
- Who is responsible for addressing them?
- How is progress being measured?
- What has changed over time?

---

## What We Are Not

National Backlog is not:

- A political movement
- A lobby group
- A protest platform
- A complaints board
- A place for outrage
- A vehicle for attacking individuals

We do not exist to assign blame.

We exist to maintain visibility and encourage progress.

---

## Our Core Principle

### No Blame. Only Ownership.

Blame looks backward.

Progress looks forward.

When a problem exists, the most important questions are:

- Is it real?
- How significant is it?
- Who owns it?
- What actions are underway?
- Are outcomes improving?

National Backlog focuses on solutions, accountability, and measurable progress.

---

## Our Philosophy

We borrow ideas from engineering, operations, and continuous improvement.

When an incident occurs in a technology environment, we:

- Identify it
- Record it
- Prioritize it
- Assign ownership
- Define metrics
- Track remediation
- Review progress

We do not forget incidents because new incidents appear.

We maintain a backlog until work is complete.

National challenges deserve the same discipline.

---

## Using Technology to Improve Visibility

Modern technology allows us to move beyond opinions, anecdotes, and news cycles.

National Backlog will use data, open information, automation, visualisation, artificial intelligence, and collaborative platforms to continuously monitor trends, track progress, identify emerging risks, and highlight areas requiring attention.

Rather than relying solely on periodic reports or public attention, we seek to create a living system that helps citizens understand:

- What is improving
- What is deteriorating
- What risks are emerging
- Where intervention may be needed
- Whether actions are producing measurable outcomes

Technology should help us see the nation's challenges more clearly, not make them more complex.

Better visibility leads to better decisions.

## How Issues Are Tracked

Every issue should contain:

### Description
What is the problem?

### Impact
How many people are affected?

### Priority
How serious is the issue?

### Ownership
Which institutions are responsible?

### Metrics
How will progress be measured?

### Trend
Improving, stable, or deteriorating?

### Risks
What emerging risks could worsen the situation?

### Evidence
What data supports the assessment?

### Updates
What has changed since the last review?

### Status
Open, Monitoring, Improving, or Resolved.

## Our Rules

### Rule 1: Evidence Before Opinion

Claims should be supported by data, research, official reports, or credible sources.

Opinions are welcome.

Evidence is required.

---

### Rule 2: Outcomes Matter More Than Announcements

A funding announcement is not success.

A strategy document is not success.

Success is measured by improved outcomes.

---

### Rule 3: Everything Remains Visible

Problems do not disappear because discussion disappears.

Issues remain on the backlog until measurable progress is achieved.

Historical records are preserved.

---

### Rule 4: Neutrality Matters

National Backlog belongs to everyone.

The purpose is not to promote ideology.

The purpose is to track reality.

---

### Rule 5: Transparency Matters

Methods, rankings, metrics, and changes should be visible and auditable.

Trust comes from openness.

## The National Scoreboard

Every quarter, National Backlog should answer one simple question:

### Are We Making Progress?

Example:

| Issue | Trend |
|---------|---------|
| Housing Affordability | 🔴 Worse |
| Regional GP Access | 🟠 Stable |
| Cyber Resilience | 🟢 Improving |
| Teacher Shortages | 🔴 Worse |
| Water Security | 🟠 Stable |

The objective is not to create winners and losers.

The objective is to maintain a shared understanding of where the nation is improving and where attention is still required.

## Building a National Memory

Modern news cycles excel at highlighting what happened this week.

National Backlog exists to remember what still needs fixing.

A challenge should not disappear from public attention simply because a newer story takes its place.

Progress requires continuity.

Accountability requires memory.

Improvement requires measurement.

---

## Our Long-Term Vision

Imagine a future where:

- Citizens can see the nation's priorities in one place.
- Journalists can track progress over decades.
- Researchers can contribute evidence.
- Governments can demonstrate measurable outcomes.
- Election cycles no longer erase institutional memory.
- Emerging risks are identified before they become crises.
- Technology helps surface trends that would otherwise remain invisible.

Instead of asking:

"What is today's headline?"

We can ask:

"What are Australia's most important unresolved challenges, and are we making progress?"

- Evidence-driven
- Transparent
- Constructive

- Accessible
- Focused on outcomes

## Closing Statement

Every successful enterprise maintains a backlog, a risk register, performance indicators, ownership, and a continuous improvement process.

A nation deserves the same level of visibility.

We cannot improve what we do not measure.

We cannot sustain progress if we forget.

National Backlog uses modern technology to help Australia remember, measure, track, and improve the challenges that shape our future.
